\begin{abstract}
Running a convolutional person detector on every frame of a static-camera stream wastes most of its computation whenever the scene does not change. We revisit a hybrid system that gates YOLOv8m with frame differencing and turn it into a design with guarantees and understood failure modes. A scheduler with a refresh interval $K$ and a minimum interval $M$ bounds the age of every reported box by $K-1$ frames and keeps the fraction of frames sent to the detector between $1/K$ and $1/M$. We show that the classic difference gate suffers from a rectification bias of $\BiasCoef\,\sigma$ under sensor noise; a percolation argument predicts its breakdown at $\sigma=\PredSigmaPcFD$, and we measure $\MeasSigmaFD$. A stabilised gate that smooths signed differences, registers frames by phase correlation under an edge-based model selection, compensates illumination offsets and propagates the registration error into its threshold raises the noise limit to $\sigma=\MeasSigmaFDS$ and cuts false triggers from \GateFprFD{} to \GateFprFDS{} of the transitions on a controlled benchmark with exact ground truth. Gated by it, the detector runs on \PolRatioFDS{} of the frames while the system reproduces the every-frame output (recall \PolRecallFDS, mean IoU \PolIouFDS). At smaller budgets the rate-limited gate keeps a recall of \PolRecallFDSMFour{} where fixed-rate subsampling reaches \FixedRecallAtFDSMFour. On a busy real video the gate is active on \VtActiveFDS{} of the frames, and $M$ bounds the cost. Code, tests and a one-command reproduction accompany the paper.
\end{abstract}
